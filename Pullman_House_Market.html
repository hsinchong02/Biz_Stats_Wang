How the density and statistics are calculated

Each histogram bin has height 100 × count / (sample size × bin width). The red empirical polygon connects bin midpoints and closes at zero one bin width beyond each outer midpoint. Its total area is 100%. Polygon probabilities use exact trapezoid areas; polygon mean, variance, and standard deviation use exact integration of its linear segments.

The dark-blue curve is a fitted theoretical normal PDF: 100 × exp(−(x − m)² / (2s²)) / (s√(2π)). For each chart, m and s are the arithmetic mean and sample standard deviation (denominator n − 1) of that chart’s raw observations: parent prices in block one, group means in blocks two and three. PDF probability uses a normal CDF approximation. These fitted parameters determine the blue PDF; fitted moment cards are not displayed. A continuous normal fit is unavailable if s is zero or undefined.

Choose Both, Fitted PDF only, or Empirical polygon only above each chart. Interval probabilities follow the selected curve(s) and are labeled separately. The Mean, Variance, and Standard deviation buttons always report empirical polygon moments, regardless of which curves are visible. Only the selected numeric value is shown; selecting another statistic replaces it, and selecting the same statistic again hides it. Changing bin width refreshes any displayed empirical statistic but never changes the normal fit. Individual group Sample mean buttons always average that group’s actual saved values.

The parent includes original listing prices plus normal random draws generated using their pooled mean and sample standard deviation. This is a teaching model, not proof that real listing prices are normally distributed. Original dollar prices are truncated to thousands; simulated prices retain decimals, including any negative normal draws. Full source statistics and extraction decisions remain in the Excel workbook and extraction notes.
